10 sec

Freezing a batch

  • Large food bags, four portions each, so one bag is one dinner.
  • Squeeze the air out and freeze flat on a tray, roughly even thickness.
  • Stand them upright in a tub. Write on the top edge: CHILLI - SEPT.
  • Take from the front. Best within 3 months, still fine at 6.
  • Thaw overnight in the fridge, never from frozen.

Cooking double

Doesn't freeze

About the numbers, and where the recipes came from. None of these are family recipes or anything out of a book. They were written to fit the shape of the week (one cook session, oven timers, shift dinners) rather than copied from anywhere, and none of them have been through a proper kitchen test yet. Treat the first run of each as the test. The figures on each card are worked out from that card's own ingredient list using standard UK composition values, so they follow the recipe rather than sitting beside it, but they are still an arithmetic estimate off raw weights and not a lab measurement. Change a recipe and the figures need redoing: the sums live in tools/macros.
Everything is cooked mild. Chilli flakes stay on the table, harissa or chipotle goes into our two bowls at the end, and the hotter curry paste only into the adults' portions. The boys can have more heat when they ask for it.

Cycle 1, September

The baskets as written. Double batches, two or three bags into the freezer every week, about nine dinners banked by the end of the month. This is the point of the first month: after it, a week that falls apart costs nothing.

Cycle 2 onwards

The second slow cooker bag stops. Weeks 1, 2 and 3 make one bag instead of two, which takes 600g of chicken thigh off each of those baskets. Week 4 drops to a single batch of minestrone. The big pots keep banking one bag a week, so the freezer still grows, just slowly.

When to run a freezer week

When the tub holds eight or more bags, skip a cook session. Shop only the fresh things (bread, ham, salad, milk, fruit) and eat five bags that week, oldest first. It leaves three or four in reserve, which is the amount worth having.

Friday pizza night 15 min

Boys' margherita

  • 2 thawed dough balls (~250g each)
  • 200g passata
  • pinch salt
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 2 balls mozzarella (125g each), torn and drained on kitchen roll
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
~1075 kcal · 45g protein · 132g carbs · 38g fat · 7g fibre (per whole pizza, one 250g dough ball; the boys rarely finish a whole one, so a realistic child's share is nearer 650)
  1. Oven as hot as it goes (250C+), a flat baking tray in there heating for 15 minutes.
  2. Stretch dough on floured baking paper. Thin middle, fat edge.
  3. Passata mixed with salt and oregano, thin layer, then mozzarella.
  4. Slide paper onto the hot tray. 8-10 minutes until blistered.

Our pizza

One extra dough ball, going in the same oven on the same tray. Meat or fish on top rather than just cheese, and go easier on the mozzarella than the boys' one. The topping changes with the week and is on that week's Fri tab. The method is the same every time:

  • 1 thawed dough ball
  • thin layer of passata
  • that week's topping (Fri tab)
  • mozzarella, less than on the boys' pizza
  • olive oil
  1. Stretch, sauce, then the topping under the cheese so it crisps rather than steams. Mozzarella last.
  2. Same hot tray, 8-10 minutes. Slice, add whatever the week's recipe finishes with, half each.

Friday salad
